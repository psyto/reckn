1. The official registry refuses the agent's own feedback

We registered agent 10518 in the official ERC-8004 reference deployment on Sepolia — not our own copy of it. That is the standard doing its job, and it is also the whole problem. The refusal only moves the question: now the record says whatever that second address says. Nothing made the second address tell the truth.

2. So the record is created by a settlement instead

… resolving the record through ENS…

A buyer funds an escrow and names the verifier. Anybody may settle it by handing over a proof that the job was re-executed and reproduced — there is no resolver, no owner, no key that decides. The same settlement grants the buyer, and nobody else, the right to write one ENS text record about that job. Then the right is destroyed.

Read back through UniversalResolverV2, not by calling our resolver directly: 

…

The key names the name it belongs to, and that is not decoration. The deployed ENSv2 resolver derives a role's resource from the key alone — we measured agent.reckn.eth and victim.reckn.eth with one key returning one identical resource. A role is therefore never scoped to a name. Naming the name inside the key does not stop bytes being written onto somebody else's name; it stops them being read as that somebody's record.

3. The record is a pass to a Uniswap v4 pool

… asking the hook…

A beforeSwap hook 0x68116b80…0080 on the real Sepolia PoolManager reads the record inside the swap, synchronously, with no CCIP‑Read — a hook cannot go offchain and come back. Its address was mined so that uint160(hook) & ALL_HOOK_MASK == BEFORE_SWAP_FLAG.

Earning happens where the work happened. Spending happens here. The same swap, three times, with liquidity in the pool:

What this page does not prove

The bytes are not proof‑derived. The settlement grants the key, not the value. A buyer can write reproduced under a job whose proof said failed. What a settlement creates is the right to write one record, not the record's contents — the contents are checkable by anyone against the escrow and the proof, and they are not enforced.

The hook does not identify the swapper. It asks whether a record exists and says reproduced. Nothing on chain ties an arbitrary address to a name, so a second agent could trade behind the first one's record.

A role is scoped to the key, never to the name. Measured, and it is the resolver's behaviour rather than ours. We pin the adapter to one name and put that name inside the key, which makes a write onto a foreign name unattributable — not impossible.

Nothing here is mainnet, and nothing here is money. Sepolia, test tokens, and an escrow holding a mock USDC.
