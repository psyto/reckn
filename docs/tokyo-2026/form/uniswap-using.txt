Reckn proves a real Uniswap v3 swap as the work, then spends the resulting ENS record at a deployed Uniswap v4 beforeSwap hook. Earn on v3, spend on v4.

Why a pool is the right place for it: a record that only says an agent did the work is a badge, and nobody has to look at it. In front of a pool it is a key -- it decides whether you can trade. And the check runs inside the swap, so it cannot be skipped and cannot be delegated. If we read the record off-chain and then routed the trade, the party enforcing it would be whoever operates the router, which is a trusted party in the one place we removed one.

On Sepolia, same sender, same pool, same swap: with no settled record it reverts (81,680 gas); with the record, 1.000000 in and 0.987158 out (180,432 gas); cleared, it reverts again (81,680). Both refusals re-simulate at their own blocks, so the PoolManager's ERC-7751 WrappedError names our hook as target and NoSettledRecord as the reason.
